# 7 Common Reasons Your Septic Alarm Is Going Off

Many septic systems, especially those with a pump tank, mound system, pressure-dosed drain field, or aerobic treatment unit (ATU), include an alarm panel that warns when something is not working as expected. Most septic alarms are triggered by a float switch that rises when wastewater in the pump chamber reaches a level higher than normal. An alarm does not always mean a major failure, but it does mean the system needs attention soon. The causes below are the most common reasons a septic alarm goes off.

## 1. The Effluent Pump Has Stopped Working

The most common cause of a high-water alarm is a pump that is no longer moving wastewater out of the pump chamber to the drain field. Pumps can fail from age, wear, debris, or electrical problems. When the pump stops, wastewater keeps rising until it lifts the alarm float.

## 2. A Tripped Breaker or Power Interruption

Septic pumps and alarms are often on separate electrical circuits. If the pump's breaker trips or a power outage affects only the pump circuit, the pump cannot run while the alarm still sounds. Checking the breaker panel is a reasonable first step, but a breaker that trips repeatedly points to an electrical or pump problem that needs professional service.

## 3. A Stuck or Failed Float Switch

Float switches tell the pump when to turn on and off and tell the alarm when water is too high. A float that is tangled, stuck, coated in debris, or failing can keep the pump from turning on or trigger a false alarm even when water levels are normal.

## 4. Heavy Water Use in a Short Period

Running several loads of laundry, hosting extra guests, or using a large amount of water at once can send more wastewater into the system than the pump chamber can handle on its normal cycle. In these cases the alarm may clear on its own after water use is reduced for several hours, but repeated alarms after normal use are a sign of an underlying issue.

## 5. Heavy Rain, Snowmelt, or High Groundwater

Groundwater or surface water can enter a septic tank or pump chamber through cracked lids, unsealed risers, or damaged pipes. Saturated soil around the drain field can also slow how quickly effluent leaves the system. Alarms that appear mainly during wet seasons often point to water infiltration or drainage problems.

## 6. A Clogged Effluent Filter or Blocked Line

Many systems have an effluent filter that keeps solids from leaving the septic tank. When the filter becomes clogged, or when a line between the tank, pump chamber, and drain field is blocked, wastewater cannot move through the system normally and levels rise. Regular septic pumping and filter cleaning help prevent this.

## 7. An Aerator or Treatment Unit Malfunction

Aerobic treatment units use an air pump or aerator to support the bacteria that treat wastewater. Many ATUs include a separate alarm that sounds when the aerator stops running or air pressure drops. This type of alarm usually signals a mechanical or electrical issue rather than high water.

## Quick Answer

A septic alarm usually goes off because wastewater in the pump chamber has risen higher than normal or because a system component has stopped working. Common causes include a failed effluent pump, a tripped breaker or power interruption, a stuck float switch, heavy water use, groundwater or rainwater infiltration, a clogged effluent filter or blocked line, and an aerator or treatment unit malfunction. A septic service professional can identify the cause and recommend repair, pumping, or replacement.

## What to Do When a Septic Alarm Sounds

- **Silence the alarm.** Most alarm panels have a button that turns off the sound while leaving the warning light on.
- **Reduce water use right away.** Avoid laundry, dishwashers, showers, and extra flushing until the cause is found, since the tank has limited reserve capacity.
- **Check the pump breaker.** If the breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a professional.
- **Do not open the tank or pump chamber.** Septic tanks contain harmful gases and electrical components and should only be serviced by trained technicians.
- **Call a septic service provider.** Even if the alarm stops on its own, a professional inspection can confirm whether the pump, float, filter, or drain field needs service before a backup occurs.
