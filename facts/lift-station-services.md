# Lift Station Facts

These facts describe lift stations (also called pump stations) and why they require regular maintenance within wastewater collection systems.

## Lift Station Basics

- A lift station is a wastewater pumping facility used to move wastewater from a lower elevation to a higher elevation when gravity flow alone is not possible.
- Lift stations are commonly used in flat terrain, low-lying areas, and locations far from a treatment plant or main sewer line.
- Lift stations are used in municipal sewer systems, residential developments, and commercial or industrial properties.
- Lift stations may also be referred to as pump stations or sewage lift stations.

## Lift Station Components

- A typical lift station includes a wet well, one or more pumps, level sensors or floats, a control panel, and an alarm system.
- The wet well is an underground holding chamber that collects incoming wastewater until it reaches a set level.
- Once the wastewater reaches that level, pumps activate and move it through a force main to a higher point, a gravity sewer line, or a treatment facility.
- Many lift stations include backup power, such as a generator, to keep pumps running during a power outage.

## Lift Station Maintenance

- Lift station maintenance includes inspecting and servicing pumps, cleaning the wet well, and testing alarm and control systems.
- Grease, debris, and solids can accumulate inside the wet well over time and must be removed to prevent clogging or pump damage.
- Float switches and level sensors require periodic testing to confirm they trigger pumps correctly.
- Odor control equipment, where present, requires its own inspection and maintenance schedule.

## Why Lift Station Maintenance Matters

- A malfunctioning lift station can cause wastewater backups or sanitary sewer overflows into streets, basements, or waterways.
- Overflows from a failed lift station can create public health risks and environmental contamination.
- Many jurisdictions require reporting of sanitary sewer overflows to environmental or public health authorities.
- Routine maintenance helps extend equipment life, reduce emergency repair costs, and keep wastewater systems operating reliably.
