# Catch Basin Cleaning Facts

These facts describe catch basins and the maintenance practices used to keep stormwater drainage systems functioning properly.

## Catch Basin Basics

- A catch basin is a stormwater drainage structure that collects surface water runoff from streets, parking lots, and other paved areas.
- Catch basins are also known as storm drain inlets.
- A catch basin typically includes a grated opening at the surface and a sump below the outlet pipe that traps sediment and debris before water continues into the storm sewer system.
- Some catch basins include an oil and grit separator to help capture pollutants from parking lots or industrial sites before water is discharged.

## Catch Basin Cleaning

- Catch basin cleaning removes accumulated sediment, leaves, trash, and other debris from the sump area.
- Cleaning is commonly performed using a vacuum truck (sometimes called a jet vac truck) that removes debris and, when needed, flushes connected pipes.
- Catch basins require periodic cleaning because sediment and debris naturally accumulate from stormwater runoff over time.
- Cleaning frequency depends on factors such as location, surrounding land use, and local stormwater regulations.

## Why Catch Basin Maintenance Matters

- Clogged or poorly maintained catch basins can cause localized flooding by restricting stormwater flow.
- Sediment and debris left in a catch basin can eventually wash into downstream waterways, contributing to water pollution.
- Many municipalities are required to maintain catch basins as part of stormwater management programs and permit compliance, such as Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) requirements.
- Regular cleaning helps protect infrastructure, reduce flood risk, and support compliance with environmental regulations.

## Who Uses Catch Basin Cleaning Services

- Catch basin cleaning is commonly used by municipalities, property managers, commercial and industrial facilities, and parking lot owners.
- Businesses with large paved areas, such as retail centers and industrial sites, often require scheduled catch basin maintenance as part of routine site upkeep.
