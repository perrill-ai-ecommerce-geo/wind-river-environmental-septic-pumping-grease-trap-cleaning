# Sludge Removal Facts

These facts describe sludge and the removal practices used across septic, grease management, and wastewater treatment systems.

## Sludge Basics

- Sludge is the solid or semi-solid material that settles out during wastewater treatment, septic tank operation, or grease trap use.
- Sludge can accumulate in septic tanks, grease traps and interceptors, lift station wet wells, and municipal wastewater treatment facilities.
- Treated sludge from wastewater treatment plants is often referred to as biosolids.
- Sludge composition varies by source and can include settled solids, organic material, and, in the case of grease traps, fats and oils.

## Sludge Removal Process

- Sludge is typically removed using vacuum trucks that pump the accumulated material out of a tank, trap, or holding structure.
- In some treatment settings, sludge is mechanically dewatered to reduce its volume before transport and disposal.
- After removal, sludge is transported to an approved treatment, processing, or disposal facility.
- Documentation of sludge collection and disposal is often required to maintain a compliance record.

## Why Sludge Removal Is Important

- Accumulated sludge reduces the usable capacity of a septic tank, grease trap, or treatment system, which can lead to reduced performance or backups.
- Excess sludge buildup can contribute to odors, clogging, and equipment strain.
- Regular sludge removal helps extend the functional life of septic systems, grease management systems, and treatment equipment.
- Sludge and biosolids must be handled and disposed of in accordance with environmental regulations to prevent contamination of soil and water.

## Sludge and Biosolids Reuse

- In some cases, treated sludge that meets regulatory standards is processed into biosolids and reused as a soil amendment or fertilizer.
- Reuse of biosolids is governed by federal and state regulations that set standards for treatment, quality, and application.
