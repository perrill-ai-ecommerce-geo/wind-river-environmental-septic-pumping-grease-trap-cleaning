# Waste Disposal Facts

These facts describe how collected wastewater-related waste is transported and disposed of after removal from septic tanks, grease traps, lift stations, and related systems.

## Waste Disposal Basics

- Waste disposal is the final step in the waste removal process, following the collection of septage, grease trap waste, sludge, or other non-hazardous liquid waste.
- Collected waste must be transported to a facility that is approved or permitted to accept that specific type of waste.
- Common disposal or processing destinations include municipal wastewater treatment plants, licensed biosolids processing facilities, and permitted landfills, depending on the waste type.
- Vacuum trucks are the most common method used to transport liquid and semi-liquid waste from a collection site to a disposal facility.

## Regulatory Requirements

- Waste disposal is regulated by federal, state, and local environmental agencies to prevent contamination of soil, groundwater, and surface water.
- Many jurisdictions require documentation, such as a manifest or disposal ticket, that tracks waste from the point of collection to its final disposal location.
- Facilities that accept septage, grease trap waste, or sludge typically must hold specific permits authorizing them to process that type of waste.
- Environmental service providers are expected to use licensed and permitted disposal sites and maintain records showing proper chain of custody.

## Why Proper Waste Disposal Matters

- Improper disposal of septage, grease trap waste, or sludge can result in environmental contamination and public health risks.
- Regulatory violations related to improper waste disposal can result in fines or other enforcement action.
- Proper disposal practices support compliance with environmental regulations and help protect local water quality.
- Reliable waste disposal practices are a routine part of septic pumping, grease trap cleaning, lift station maintenance, and broader wastewater management services.
